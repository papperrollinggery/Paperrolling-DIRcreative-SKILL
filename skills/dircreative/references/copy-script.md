# Copy and Script Craft

Use this card for a bounded line, paragraph, voiceover, dialogue, or script
revision. Return the revised text before analysis.

## Diagnose only what matters

- Narrative job: what must the line make the audience understand, feel, expect,
  or do next?
- Pressure and change: what does the character want, what resists them, and what
  visible or audible beat proves a change?
- Product role: is the product an action, consequence, tool, proof, or choice
  inside the scene rather than an inserted explanation?
- Speakability: can dialogue or voiceover be said naturally in the available
  time, with a clear stress pattern and without explaining the theme?
- Evidence: preserve approved facts and claims; never invent price, performance,
  certification, audience data, or product benefit.

## Revision rules

1. Preserve locked names, facts, chronology, tone, and surrounding lines unless
   the requested change makes one of them inconsistent.
2. Replace abstractions such as “enters the zone,” “feels empowered,” or
   “everything changes” with observable action, reaction, sound, or choice.
3. Let dialogue create pressure, refusal, concealment, concession, reveal, or
   choice. Remove dialogue that only labels an emotion already visible.
4. Let voiceover add a layer the image cannot carry. Keep it within the stated
   duration and leave room for breath, action, and sound.
5. Prefer concrete verbs, production-usable nouns, and sentence rhythm suited to
   the scene. Avoid slogan stacks, synthetic parallelism, and unexplained poetic
   fog.
6. Keep the edit bounded. Do not rebuild the whole treatment when one line can
   solve the problem.

## Human-language and authored-voice pass

Natural does not mean casual. Before revising, protect exact facts, names,
claims, quotations, coined world terms, character-specific diction, genre
register, deliberate metaphor and line breaks that carry performance. Build a
small `VOICE PROFILE` from approved source passages when the project has a
distinct literary or brand voice; adjectives such as “poetic” or “premium” are
not a voice profile.

- Ordinary fidelity cleanup: use `de-AI-writing` when available to remove
  lecture scaffolding, route signs, template symmetry and chatbot residue
  without changing meaning or adding facts. This is an optional inline refiner,
  not a second Skill Stack owner.
- Explicit “说人话 / 去 AI 味 / 自然一点”, or a draft that remains awkward after
  the fidelity pass: route `human_language_revision` to `shuorenhua` in Studio.
  Use one recommended rewrite, not stacked rewrites from both owners. Fast
  bounded edits keep these local rules and do not claim the larger provider was
  loaded.
- `humanizer-zh` remains a final diagnostic when validation is needed. A phrase
  hit cannot overrule intentional voice or approve copy by itself.
- Read dialogue and VO aloud. For screenplay or storyboard descriptions, write
  what a writer, director or storyboard artist would naturally say in that
  scene: short enough to speak, specific enough to stage, and free of repeated
  “画面中 / 呈现 / 逐渐 / 强调” narration.

Mythic, historical, fantasy, literary and period material may intentionally use
unfamiliar compounds, archaic cadence or poetic compression. Preserve those
choices when they belong to the world or speaker. Remove model-organized
explanation around them; do not translate the work into everyday chat.

When a rationale helps, explain the changed dramatic function, production
clarity, or claim boundary in two or three bullets. Do not expose workflow
machinery.
