# Shot and Storyboard Craft

Use this card to improve one shot or review a small storyboard set. Lead with the
revised shot card or the highest-impact findings, not a process preamble.

## Review order

1. Narrative purpose: what changes in this shot, and why must the audience see it?
2. Visible action: one dominant action or readable beat per shot/generation unit.
3. Camera logic: size, angle, lens intent, support, and a movement with a physical
   start target and end target.
4. Blocking: subject start, path, end, eyeline, screen direction, and axis when
   continuity depends on them.
5. Composition: foreground/midground/background, subject hierarchy, movement
   room, readable face/product/text zone, and what stays locked during motion.
6. Sound and edit: dialogue/VO, ambience, SFX, music or silence, plus the cut or
   transition motivation.
7. Continuity and model risk: identity, wardrobe, prop hand, product orientation,
   geography, lighting, state changes, likely model misread, and whether to split.

## Useful shot-card shape

```text
S03 00:08-00:12 | <narrative purpose>
Frame/camera: <size, angle, lens intent, support, start -> end>
Blocking/action: <subject start -> path/action -> end, eyeline/axis>
Composition/light: <layers, hierarchy, readable zone, light change>
Continuity: <identity, prop, direction, state locks>
Sound/edit: <audio layers and cut point>
Model risk: <likely misread and smallest prevention/split rule>
```

For a review, rank only material problems, explain their audience or production
impact, and provide a concrete replacement. Do not turn a few frames into a full
project gate. Do not invent missing wardrobe, product, location, or source facts;
mark the exact unknown that changes the shot.
