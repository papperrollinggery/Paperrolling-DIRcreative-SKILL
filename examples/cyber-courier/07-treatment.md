## Treatment

Night rain turns the city into a field of reflections. At the edge of a narrow service alley, a young courier in a red waterproof jacket stops under broken neon. The street behind him still feels public: traffic hum, signage, passing light. The alley ahead feels private and mechanical, a blue-black cut between buildings where the pavement disappears into steam.

He checks the black delivery bag against his side. The address is correct. That is the problem. Nobody steps into this alley by mistake, and nobody who knows the district wants to enter it on purpose. He looks once toward the street, then back into the alley, making the small professional decision to continue.

The courier moves forward without running. Rain beads on his jacket and face. His grip tightens around the bag strap as red signage on the left side of the alley flickers across the wet ground. A blue `24小时` sign deeper in the frame gives the space a false sense of normal service, but the closed metal doors and exposed pipes make it feel like the city has turned its back.

Inside the delivery bag, the sealed package wakes before he reaches the destination. A red scanner mark pulses under the waterproof wrapping. The glow is not random; it matches the red sign behind him, as if the package and alley are part of the same circuit. For the first time, the delivery stops feeling like an errand and starts feeling like a key being carried to a lock.

He looks up. The performance is restrained: no shouted fear, no sudden sprint, only a held breath, a tighter jaw, and a decision forming behind tired eyes. The alley seems to notice him noticing it. Rain and neon continue, but the space feels quieter.

At the far end, a sealed metal service door comes alive. A thin line of light appears around the intercom panel. The courier stops short of the door with the bag still in his hand. The package glow reflects in the water at his feet. The film ends before anyone opens the door, leaving the sense that the delivery has already succeeded in the worst possible way: it has awakened the address.

## Pacing

The first three seconds should establish the forbidden threshold. The middle five seconds should make the courier's commitment and the package reaction clear. The final seven seconds should slow down slightly so the reaction and door activation land as a suspense beat, not a random effect.

## Sound Intent

No dialogue or voiceover is required. The story should survive as a silent visual sequence. Later video adapters may add rain ambience, distant traffic hum, neon electrical buzz, and one scanner beep at the object reveal.

## What Must Stay Out

- No explanation of who sent the package.
- No visible recipient.
- No chase or fight.
- No extra characters.
- No exposition text.
- No image or video prompt language in this phase.
